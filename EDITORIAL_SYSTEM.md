# Digital Renaissance Editorial System

## Default publishing cadence
- Daily by default.
- 500–800 words is the standard article length.
- 50–150 words for short grenades.
- 150–400 words for sharp observations.
- 800–1,200+ words only when an idea genuinely deserves a manifesto.
- No filler to hit a word count.

## Voice
Dark. Gritty. Aggressive. Provocative. Intellectually confrontational. Occasionally abrasive, taboo, or outrageous, but never edgy for its own sake. The writing should feel like an underground magazine, field manual, or manifesto, not LinkedIn content, generic self-help, SEO sludge, or corporate thought leadership.

Every paragraph must earn its place.

## Editorial universe
1. The Machine: AI, automation, algorithms, surveillance, Big Tech, digital dependence.
2. The Grind: work, careers, hustle culture, productivity, entrepreneurship, money.
3. The Mind: psychology, attention, dopamine, identity, consciousness, self-improvement.
4. The Body: biohacking, longevity, fitness, nutrition, supplements, performance science, recovery, human optimisation.
5. The Market: capitalism, consumerism, wealth, status, financial independence, incentives.
6. The Human: relationships, loneliness, masculinity, sex, friendship, ageing, death, meaning, belonging.
7. The Taboo: socially uncomfortable questions, drugs, sex, death, censorship, power, hypocrisy, forbidden ideas. Investigate rather than sensationalise.
8. The Underground: internet subcultures, fringe communities, forgotten movements, weird history, counterculture, alternative lifestyles.
9. The State: institutions, bureaucracy, education, healthcare, regulation, government, power.
10. The Renaissance: philosophy, sovereignty, independence, creation, alternative systems, escaping default life.
11. Off the Map: genuinely strange subjects that don't need to fit anywhere else.

## Topic strategy
Rotate territories so the publication never becomes repetitive. Cross-pollinate categories aggressively: AI × psychology, biohacking × identity, capitalism × loneliness, technology × human augmentation, work × mortality, etc.

## Core test
The reader should finish thinking: "Fuck. I hadn't looked at it that way."

If a paragraph can be removed without weakening the argument, remove it.

If the piece has nothing worth saying, don't publish it.

## Safety and evidence
For health, biohacking, drugs, psychology, politics, technology, and other sensitive topics, distinguish evidence from speculation. Provocation is welcome. Unsupported certainty isn't.
